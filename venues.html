FOR RESTAURANTS, BREWERIES & COMMUNITY VENUES

You choose the slow times. We help put a recurring group at the table.

You are not signing up to run D&D. You simply tell us when you are willing to have a tabletop group, how many people fit, and your normal spending/table rules. Nearby Game Masters whose schedules match can then request that slot.

THE BUSINESS PITCH

We are trying to bring you customers during times you choose.

The first restaurant conversation should be about predictable groups and repeat visits—not RPG rules.

📅

You control the window

List only nights and hours where a 4–8 person group makes sense for your operation.

👥

We organize the group

The GM owns the game and player coordination. Your staff does not need to teach, referee, or provide gaming supplies.

🍔

You set the spending rule

Require normal purchases, a minimum per guest, a party package, or no special policy at all.

HOW A TABLE GETS CREATED

The GM schedule comes first.

1

GM posts availability

Example: Tuesday evenings and Saturdays after 5 PM, within 20 miles.

2

We find overlapping venue windows

The GM sees nearby restaurants that are actually willing and available at those times.

3

GM chooses a slot and publishes the game

Only then does the event appear to nearby players whose own availability and travel range fit.

WHAT THE VENUE DOES

Very little.

  • Choose specific available table windows.
  • Set maximum group size.
  • State food/drink or minimum-purchase expectations.
  • Provide accurate accessibility and age/alcohol information.
  • Approve or decline a GM's requested slot.

WHAT THE GM DOES

The GM owns game night.

  • Creates and accurately describes the game.
  • Manages player seats and table expectations.
  • Arrives on time and follows venue rules.
  • Keeps the group inside the confirmed time window.
  • Acts as the main point of contact for the table.

LOW-RISK PILOT

Our easiest restaurant pitch: try one table, one slow night, for four weeks.

No long contract. No staff training. No promise of permanent space. If the group behaves well and spends money, keep it. If it does not work, stop offering the slot.
