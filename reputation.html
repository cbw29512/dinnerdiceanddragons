VENUE REPUTATION IS DIFFERENT

We rate the tabletop-hosting experience—not the restaurant as a whole.

Only eligible people from a completed DDD session can submit venue feedback. Public results are structured aggregates; private notes do not become a Yelp-style review wall.

What can count publicly

  • Reserved table available as promised
  • Space suitable for tabletop play
  • Group treated as welcome customers
  • Environment/noise description accurate
  • Accessibility information accurate
  • Would play here again

What cannot become a public rating

  • Unrelated restaurant complaints
  • Anonymous drive-by reviews
  • Food reviews unrelated to table hosting
  • Political or personal disputes
  • One angry free-text accusation
  • Reports still under moderation review

Anti-pile-on controls

  • Completed-event eligibility
  • One response per person/event
  • Verified identity
  • Minimum sample thresholds
  • Anomaly/review-bomb checks
  • Venue correction/appeal path

FAIR-START GUARANTEE

A first-time user or venue should still be able to get a first table.

The ranking system must be audited for newcomer disadvantage.

Hard rules

  • No history = neutral.
  • No missing metric is displayed as 0%.
  • New users and venues lose no Table Fit points.
  • Reputation is not an ordinary eligibility requirement.
  • One negative interaction does not automatically trigger a public caution label.
  • Public aggregates require minimum sample sizes.
  • Safety restrictions are handled through moderation, not popularity ranking.

THE PRINCIPLE

Reputation reduces uncertainty. It does not decide who deserves to play—or invite a mob to punish a venue.

Table Match gets people to the opportunity. Verified history helps them understand what has happened before.
