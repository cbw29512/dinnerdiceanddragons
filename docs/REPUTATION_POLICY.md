# Dinner, Dice & Dragons — Reputation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how verified platform history may be summarized publicly without turning reputation into a barrier for newcomers.

## Core rule

**No history is neutral.**

A new Player, GM, or Venue is not treated as unreliable merely because Dinner, Dice & Dragons has not observed enough activity yet.

## Public history states

### New to DDD
Use when the platform lacks enough verified history for meaningful aggregates.

Show:
- role
- self-reported experience, clearly labeled
- `No verified platform history yet`

Do not show:
- zero-star ratings
- `0% reliability`
- warning colors solely because history is missing
- negative match adjustments

### Building History
Use when verified platform activity exists but sample thresholds are not yet met for some aggregates.

Show objective counts when appropriate, such as verified sessions attended/hosted.

Hide percentages that would be misleading at low sample sizes.

### Established
Use when enough eligible verified evidence exists to show meaningful aggregate signals.

### Caution
May be shown only when a published, evidence-based reliability threshold is met. It is not triggered by one complaint or one negative review.

## Initial sample-threshold proposal

For prototype/policy testing only:

- 0–4 eligible responses: do not publish a would-repeat percentage
- 5+ eligible responses: percentage may be shown with response count

Example:

`91% would play again · 27 eligible responses`

The denominator must always be available to the UI.

## Matching policy

Reputation is not a hard constraint for ordinary Table Match.

Matching order:

1. required logistical constraints
2. table/preference compatibility
3. verified reliability context only after viable matches exist

A newcomer with excellent fit may rank above an established user with materially worse logistics or table fit.

## Attendance policy

Attendance records distinguish:
- attended
- excused absence
- late cancellation
- no-show

An excused absence does not count as a no-show.

Do not reduce a public reliability metric from a cancellation when the platform/venue/GM caused the cancellation.

## Feedback policy

Reputation-bearing feedback requires an eligible completed-event relationship.

One person may not submit multiple reputation-bearing responses about the same subject for the same event unless the product explicitly defines multiple independent dimensions in one response.

Free-text feedback is private by default.

## Moderation separation

Reports and allegations do not automatically alter public reputation.

Moderators may apply account restrictions based on safety policy and evidence. These restrictions are authorization/moderation state, not a popularity score.

## Appeals and correction

Production moderation/reputation systems must support correcting authoritative source records when attendance or event status is demonstrably wrong.

Derived reputation snapshots are then recalculated from corrected evidence.

## Fair-start audit

Regularly compare newcomer and established-user outcomes after controlling for eligibility and Table Fit.

Watch:
- impressions per eligible match
- invitations/requests per eligible match
- confirmation rate
- played-session rate

If new users are systematically disadvantaged because of history state rather than fit, adjust ranking/presentation logic.
